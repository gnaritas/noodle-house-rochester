Our story · Broadway & 4th · est. November 2026

“It feels like a noodle house.”

That’s what Marlene Rachwat told the Post Bulletin the first time she saw the little space on South Broadway. Eight months of renovation and one name later, The Noodle House is where she and Chef Chai serve the dishes their other menu never had room for.

See the menu Call (507) 206-0577 Visit us

Chai and Marlene run Mango Thai at 318 South Broadway, a few doors north. When the neighboring space came open, the pull was immediate — not for another Thai restaurant, but for the single thing the two of them keep coming back to: noodles.

“Chai and I just love noodles, like Pho, Thai noodles and Ramen. We can’t put all of the noodle dishes we like on the menu at Mango Thai. This is the perfect place to serve all of the kinds of noodles that we love,” Marlene said — and that is the whole business plan, in her words.

The building

The Noodle House sits inside the Kelley Building, Rochester’s first brick building — raised in 1858 by Dr. Lewis Halsey Kelley with locally made bricks, and home over the years to the city’s first medical practice, a dry goods store, a basement saloon and, up the stairs, the first newspaper. It takes the corner of South Broadway and Fourth Street SE.

The opening

The space before was Marrow, the 40-seat fine-dining room chefs Jeff and Sarah Schwenker closed in May. The Rachwats took it over for one purpose, announced it in their own words — “The wait is over. Noodle House is opening its doors, and we can’t wait to welcome you” — and soft-opened on Sunday, November 8, 2026, serving noon to eight.
