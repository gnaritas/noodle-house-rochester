Broth bowls · pho & ramen

The pot that takes all day.

Pho and ramen are built the same way: broth simmered long enough to taste like it was. At The Noodle House they’re the two bowls the menu was founded on — rice noodles for pho, wheat for ramen, both swimming in whatever the pot has been doing since morning.

Ask what’s in the pot · (507) 206-0577 See the whole menu

“Chai and I just love noodles, like Pho, Thai noodles and Ramen,” Marlene Rachwat told the Post Bulletin when the restaurant was still an empty space on Broadway. “We can’t put all of the noodle dishes we like on the menu at Mango Thai. This is the perfect place to serve all of the kinds of noodles that we love.”

So the broth bowls get their own house. Pho arrives the Vietnamese way — rice noodles, deeply savory broth, herbs and the sharp fixings alongside for you to build your bowl. Ramen runs the Japanese lane: a different broth, a different noodle, the same patience.

The specifics — which broth on which day, at what price — are the kitchen’s call and printed at the restaurant. What’s promised is the two bowls the owners named to KROC News as the anchor of the menu.
